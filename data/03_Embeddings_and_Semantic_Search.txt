EMBEDDINGS AND SEMANTIC SEARCH

Overview
An embedding is a numerical representation of information. Text embeddings convert words, sentences, or documents into vectors so that a computer can compare their meaning mathematically.

Vector Representation
A text item is represented by a list of numerical values called a vector. Texts with related meanings tend to have vectors that are close to each other in an embedding space.

Similarity
Cosine similarity is commonly used to compare vectors. A higher similarity generally means that two text representations are more closely related in meaning.

Semantic Search
Semantic search retrieves information according to meaning rather than requiring an exact keyword match. For example, a question about automobile costs may retrieve a passage that uses the term vehicle expenses.

Embedding Models
Sentence-transformer models are commonly used to generate sentence and paragraph embeddings. The model transforms text into fixed-length vectors that can be stored in a vector database.

Advantages
Semantic search can handle synonyms, related concepts, and natural-language questions better than simple keyword matching.

Conclusion
Embeddings provide the numerical foundation for many modern search, recommendation, and retrieval systems.
